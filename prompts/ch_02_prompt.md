Chapter 2 — Recommend a Software Process for a Project Scenario 

1. Role 

You are a senior software process engineer and systems analyst evaluating how a software project should be organized. Prioritize correctness, clarity of trade-offs, maintainability, and fit to project context over generic or one-size-fits-all advice. 

2. Context 

The project scenario is as follows: 

System/application type: [describe the system] 

Team size and skills: [team details] 

Requirements stability: [stable / somewhat changing / highly volatile] 

Delivery expectations: [single release / staged rollout / frequent increments] 

Compliance or documentation needs: [low / moderate / high] 

Reuse opportunities: [existing components, COTS, frameworks, legacy systems] 

Customer involvement: [low / periodic / continuous] 

Relevant software process concepts from Chapter 2: 

All software processes involve specification, design and implementation, validation, and evolution. 

Common process models include waterfall, incremental development, and reuse-oriented development. 

Plan-driven approaches work better when requirements are well understood and changes are limited. 

Agile/incremental approaches help when requirements are expected to change and early feedback is valuable. 

Prototyping and incremental delivery help reduce the cost of rework and cope with changing requirements. 

Process improvement should consider measurement, analysis, and change, guided by organizational objectives. 

3. Task / Objective 

Analyze the project scenario and recommend the most appropriate software process model or hybrid approach. Explain how the chosen process will handle requirements, design/implementation, validation, evolution, and change over time. 

4. Requirements 

Functional 

Identify whether the project is best suited to a plan-driven, agile/incremental, reuse-oriented, or hybrid process. 

Justify the recommendation using the project context. 

Explain how the process will address: 

Requirements engineering 

Design and implementation 

Validation/testing 

Evolution/change management 

State whether prototyping, incremental delivery, or software reuse/COTS integration should be used. 

Include a short discussion of likely risks, trade-offs, and limitations of the chosen approach. 

Propose a simple process improvement plan using measurable indicators. 

Non-functional 

Keep the recommendation structured, concise, and evidence-based. 

Ground all conclusions in the scenario rather than assuming ideal conditions. 

Do not present any process as universally best; emphasize context-sensitive trade-offs. 

Use terminology consistently with Chapter 2 concepts. 

5. Constraints 

Do not assume requirements are fully stable unless the scenario explicitly says so. 

Do not recommend a pure waterfall process unless you justify why change is limited and coordination/documentation needs are high. 

Do not recommend agile/incremental development without addressing visibility, documentation, and architectural integrity. 

Do not recommend reuse-oriented development unless you discuss requirement compromises and loss of control over reused components. 

Keep the analysis within the scope of software process selection and improvement, not detailed coding or implementation. 

6. Process 

Approach the task as follows: 

Reason step by step about the scenario before making a recommendation. 

Briefly compare at least two viable process options. 

Select the best option and justify why it fits better than the alternatives. 

Work from the Chapter 2 framework: 

specification 

design and implementation 

validation 

evolution 

Include how the process will cope with change, especially through prototyping, incremental development, or staged delivery when appropriate. 

After drafting the answer, self-check it against the Requirements and revise if anything is missing. 

If information is missing from the scenario, make the minimum necessary assumptions and label them clearly. 

7. Output Format 

Deliver the answer in the following structure: 

Recommended Process Model 

Why This Fits the Scenario 

Comparison with Other Viable Options 

How the Process Handles Core Activities 

Requirements engineering 

Design and implementation 

Validation/testing 

Evolution/change 

Use of Prototyping, Incremental Delivery, and/or Reuse 

Key Risks and Trade-offs 

Process Improvement Plan 

metrics to track 

bottlenecks to watch 

proposed changes over time 

8. Verification 

This is complete when: 

The recommendation clearly matches the project context. 

At least two process options are considered and compared. 

The response explicitly addresses specification, design/implementation, validation, and evolution. 

The answer explains how the team will cope with changing requirements. 

At least three process metrics are proposed, such as: 

time taken for activities 

effort/person-days 

number of defects discovered 

The trade-offs of the chosen approach are stated honestly. 

The final answer is specific enough that a team could use it as the basis for a project process decision.
